About the Data

What the map covers, where the data come from, and how courses, educators and groups are mapped to the CFQT framework.

Scope

The map covers physics A at upper secondary school (stx and htx) and quantum-relevant university courses at five Danish universities: DTU, the University of Copenhagen (KU), Aarhus University (AU), the University of Southern Denmark (SDU) and Aalborg University (AAU). It includes bachelor, master and PhD courses and links them to the people and research groups that teach them.

It is published by Danish Quantum Academy as part of the mapping task of the European Quantum Academy (Northern European Regional Quantum Academy). All CFQT tags and levels are first estimates and will be checked with course responsibles and educators through the European Quantum Academy survey.

Upper secondary schools (stx and htx)

The STX/HTX button on the front page shows physics A at upper secondary school. It is not a list of school courses: each entry is content that applies to every school of that type, 144 stx and 34 htx schools. It has its own button and is also counted in "Denmark", together with the universities.

  • Core content. The subject guidelines for Fysik A (stx and htx, 2024) name quantum physics in the core content: photons, spectra and atomic structure on stx, photon energy and the hydrogen spectrum on htx.
  • Kvant21. A four-year project of the Danish physics teachers' association with NQCP (Niels Bohr Institute) and DTU Physics, funded by the Novo Nordisk Foundation, develops material and teacher training in quantum physics and quantum technology for physics A and B on stx and htx (KVANT, November 2025). Danish Quantum Academy counts it as required on stx Fysik A, in the 3.g topic "Fysik i det 21. århundrede", and optional on htx. The material is being developed in 2025–2027; the B-level part follows later and is not on the map yet.
  • Physics A in numbers. The number of students graduating with Fysik A and of school departments with at least one such student come from the Ministry of Children and Education's statistics on graduates' grades (uddannelsesstatistik.dk), read for 2025 and 2026.

Courses

Course lists are harvested from the universities' course catalogues and the national PhD course portal. Each course is assigned up to three CFQT subdomains, one of them primary (the course's main topic), and an estimated proficiency level for each proficiency area it addresses, based on the course description, study level and workload. Subdomains are always listed in numeric order; the primary one is shown filled.

For research groups with a publication check (so far QMATH, November 2026), the members' research proficiency is based on their five newest research outputs registered on their own university research profile. Each of the five is classified on the CFQT content map from its title, abstract and keywords, and the group page shows the five with the reason for each classification. Output found only in ORCID, OpenAlex or arXiv is listed on the group page but does not count until the researcher adds it to the profile. arXiv is searched by the member's full name as a control source (physics, mathematics and computer-science categories since 2021); titles renamed on publication are merged with the published work, and hits that clearly belong to another person with the same name are left out by hand. The check is repeated once a semester, or when a researcher asks.

In November 2026 every classification was checked in reverse: for each assigned subdomain we read the official course description and asked whether the course covers the topics CFQT lists for that subdomain (yes: two or more topics, partly: one, no: none). Subdomains with no match were replaced by the ones the description points to, and courses with no CFQT match at all were taken off the map. Of 294 courses, 65 were reclassified and 6 removed; the verdicts are kept in data/review/cfqt_check.tsv.

On the coverage page, small bars next to each course show how often quantum terms (strong) and related technical terms (weak) occur in its description. They helped select courses and are not a quality measure.

Proficiency levels and the triangle

Levels follow the six CFQT levels from A1 (awareness) to C2 in three proficiency areas: (I) Quantum concepts, (II) QT hardware and software engineering, and (III) QT applications and strategies. Courses on enabling technologies such as classical optics or nanofabrication often have no QT-specific proficiency; they appear in the content map but not in the triangle. On the front page, the shade of a band counts the courses at exactly that level in five fixed steps (1–2, 3–5, 6–12, 13–29, 30+), so the same shade means the same count for every university.

Study planner: programmes and study paths

  • Dedicated quantum master's programmes follow their curriculum. The other paths are Danish Quantum Academy's examples of a quantum-focused route through a broader programme.
  • Course roles (compulsory, restricted elective, elective) are taken from the 2026 curricula: KU study regulations, the DTU course base, AU programme structures, SDU study plans and AAU study regulations.
  • For each proficiency area, the profile reaches the highest level among the courses in the path. Shades count how many courses reach at least that level: one course, 2–5 courses, or 6 or more.
  • Master's paths assume a thesis in a quantum topic at C1 in area I, the example CFQT gives. Bachelor paths assume a bachelor project in a quantum topic at B2 in area I, with the project size the curriculum gives (10–20 ECTS). Levels reached only through the thesis or project are striped.
  • The comparison profiles are the nine CFQT qualification profiles P1–P9 (CFQT v3.0, pp. 19–27). Where the framework gives a range such as "C1 or C2", the outline uses the lower level.

Study planner: interests and electives

When a programme is chosen, the planner keeps its compulsory quantum courses and fills the room for electives (the size of the path's own electives, within what the chosen thesis length leaves) with courses that match the student's interests, preferring the restricted electives and electives the curriculum lists for the path; levels reached only through added electives are drawn paler. Without a programme, it suggests a complete quantum part of a master's programme from one university. Plans for KU or DTU include courses at both, since the two are close enough to follow courses at both. Optionally, the planner adds the fewest courses that lift a plan to a chosen CFQT qualification profile, one area at a time, choosing the course with the best match to the student's interests and the fewest ECTS. The student's text is matched against a list of topics in English and Danish that are linked to CFQT subdomains, and against course titles. Courses are scored by how well their subdomains match. The three suggestions follow the interests most closely, lean towards hands-on engineering, or lean towards theory. The timetable uses each course's semester, teaching block, weekly schedule slot (DTU modules and KU schedule groups) and recommended prerequisites from the 2026/27 course catalogues. The suggestions are not study advice.

Educators

Educators are those named in the 2026/27 course catalogues. Their position, department, group and centre come from their profiles in the universities' research portals (DTU Orbit, University of Copenhagen research portal, AU Pure, SDU Find Researcher, AAU VBN), harvested in November 2026. Portal fingerprint concepts are generated automatically by the portals and can contain odd terms. The research focus and its CFQT tags are written by Danish Quantum Academy from the portal data.

The fit note for each course compares the course's CFQT subdomains with those of the educator's research. It describes overlap in subject, not teaching quality.

For each educator, the research portal also gives the latest research output (titles, types and years, linked to the portal) and the ongoing PhD projects the educator supervises as main supervisor; projects where the educator is co-supervisor or a member are left out.

Research proficiency. The research of each educator is placed on the CFQT proficiency triangle from three kinds of evidence: the research profile (the research focus and CFQT tags written by Danish Quantum Academy), the research output since 2021 and the supervised PhD theses since 2021. Research output is taken from OpenAlex, the open bibliographic database, matched on the educator's name and university, because the publication lists of the research portals sit behind a bot check; people with common names can therefore have a few outputs too many or too few. Supervised PhD theses come from the "Supervised work" tab of the research portals (KU, SDU, AAU); at DTU the ongoing PhD projects as main supervisor are used; for AU the portal could not be read automatically. Each output and thesis gets up to three CFQT subdomains from its title by keyword rules (scripts/cfqt_classify.py); compared with the hand-made classification of the PhD and postdoc projects, the rules agree on whether a title is on the content map for about 9 in 10 titles and on the proficiency area of the main topic for about 8 in 10. Editorials, prefaces and similar items are left out. The main topic's area counts at C2 Innovation, other areas at C1 Specialisation; an educator reaches C2 in an area with at least three pieces of evidence whose main topic lies there, C1 with one or two (or at least two that touch the area) and B2 with a single one that touches it.

Only professional information is shown: name, position, units, research and teaching. Contact details are not published.

PhD and postdoc projects

Two sources: the ongoing PhD projects that educators supervise as main supervisor (DTU Orbit, the only research portal that shows the supervisor role), and the PhD students and postdocs in the research groups with their current project in the portal. Where a group member has no registered project, the latest research output stands in for it. Danish Quantum Academy tagged every project with up to three CFQT subdomains from its title, the first being the main topic; projects outside the content map are counted separately. On the front page, each project is placed in the proficiency triangle at C2 (Innovation) in the area of its main topic and at C1 in other areas it touches; areas follow the subdomains: (I) domains 1–2 and 5.1, 5.4, 5.5; (III) 5.6, 6.7, 7.4, 7.6 and domain 8; (II) all others. Each university card on the front page shows its project triangle below its course triangle; the projects also have their own columns in the coverage table and their own study levels (PhD projects, postdoc projects) in the list. The tags are in data/projects/cfqt_projects.txt.

Workshops and public outreach

The STX/HTX card on the front page has two triangles for activities aimed at schools and the public. Workshops are offers for upper-secondary classes, students and teachers: the Quantum Training Lab at the Niels Bohr Institute (class visits and SRP/SOP projects), the KVANT21 teaching materials and teacher training, the NQCP education team's talent programmes and the Erasmus+ project Quantum Teaching, and the school collaboration of SDU's Center for Quantum Mathematics. Public outreach is talks, events and media for the general public: the outreach list of the QUSCOPE centre at Aarhus University (2015–2020) and, every Monday, the public quantum events found in the event calendars of the Niels Bohr Institute and of the Department of Physics and Astronomy at AU (an event counts when its title is on the CFQT content map and it is marked as public). Each activity gets CFQT subdomains and a proficiency level per area, written by Danish Quantum Academy: school workshops typically reach A2 Literacy to B1 Utilisation, public talks A1 Awareness to A2 Literacy. The list is in data/outreach/activities.txt; the calendar results in data/outreach/calendar.json. The mapping is incomplete: workshops and outreach that are not described on public pages are missing.

Industry

The organisations come from The Danish Quantum Ecosystem Map of the Danish Business Authority and the Danish Quantum Community (updated 11 November 2025; list view read on 4 November 2026): quantum hardware, software and enabling-technology suppliers, test and fabrication facilities, end users, university hubs, funders, support organisations and government. Danish Quantum Academy wrote a one-line summary of each, its role in the value chain and the CFQT subdomains of its work. Companies were checked in the Central Business Register (CVR) for start date, place and industry code; contact details and people are not stored.

On the proficiency triangle, a company that develops quantum technology is placed at C2 Innovation in the area of its main topic and at C1 in other areas; test and fabrication facilities at C1; end users exploring quantum technology at B2 in applications. Funders, government and support organisations are listed but not placed. Job ads come from Jobindex search feeds for quantum terms, read every Monday; each ad gets CFQT subdomains from its title and a demanded level from the role (research roles C2, senior and specialist roles C1, engineers and developers B2, technicians and assistants B1, other roles A2). Ads are kept with the dates they were seen, so the demand builds up over time; ads from universities are shown apart. The research output of the companies and the Danish universities they publish with are read from OpenAlex in nightly runs. Data: data/industry/.

Research groups

Group profiles combine each group's own website with its unit in the university's research portal. Members, projects and recent work were harvested on . News, events, grants and open positions are fetched automatically from the groups' websites every Monday (last run ); sources that cannot be read automatically are marked on the page. Summaries and CFQT tags are written by Danish Quantum Academy; photos and long texts are not copied.

Research proficiency of the groups. Each group card and profile shows a proficiency triangle in which every member with research on the CFQT content map counts once in each area, at their level there, by the same rule as for the educators. The evidence is the member's research output since 2021 from OpenAlex (matched on name and university), the member's current project in the research portal and, for educators, the evidence on the Educators page. Technical and administrative staff are not counted. OpenAlex limits the number of requests per day, so the members are read in nightly runs until all are covered.
