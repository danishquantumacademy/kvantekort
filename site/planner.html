Quantum Study Planner

Choose your programme, see the CFQT proficiency profile it gives you, and add quantum electives that fit what you find interesting.

How it works

1 · Your programme. Choose a bachelor's or master's programme and the length of the thesis or bachelor project; it starts at the length the curriculum gives. Dedicated quantum programmes follow their curriculum; the example paths are Danish Quantum Academy's suggestions for a quantum-focused route through a broader programme. The triangle shows the profile a graduate reaches with the quantum-relevant courses of the path and, if included, a thesis or bachelor project in a quantum topic. Choose "No programme yet" to get suggestions from all master's courses at a university instead.

2 · Your interests. Your text is matched against a list of topics (in English and Danish) that are linked to CFQT subdomains, and against course titles. The planner keeps the compulsory courses of your programme and fills the room for electives (the size of the path's own electives, within what the thesis leaves) with courses that match your interests, preferring the electives the curriculum lists. You get three variants: closest to your interests, more hands-on and more theory.

Triangles. Shades show how many courses reach each level (light 1, medium 2–5, dark 6 or more). Levels reached only through the electives you add are drawn paler; levels reached only through the thesis or project are striped. The dark outline is the CFQT qualification profile you compare with.

3 · Reach a CFQT profile. Choose one of the nine CFQT qualification profiles (P1–P9). It is drawn as an outline in every triangle. Tick "Suggest courses that close the gaps" to add, area by area, the course that lifts the plan to the profile's level with the best match to your interests and the fewest ECTS; added courses are marked "for P6" (or the profile you chose). If no course in the selection reaches a level, the planner says so.

KU and DTU. The two universities are close enough to follow courses at both, so a plan for one always includes courses at the other.

Compare. Choose a second programme to see its standard profile next to yours.

Timetable. For master's plans, the timetable uses each course's semester, teaching block, weekly schedule slot (DTU modules and KU schedule groups) and recommended prerequisites from the 2026/27 course catalogues.

These are suggestions, not study advice. Check your curriculum for which courses count towards your degree, and talk to your study counsellor. Method and sources: About the data.
